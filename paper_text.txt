The Certified Wrong Function: Measuring Joint Silence in
Form-Specification Gates for Physical Model Discovery
Anonymous — double-blind review copy
Abstract
Symbolic regression and physical model discovery pipelines certify candidate equations with goodness-of-fit metrics
— R², cross-validation error, information criteria, coefficient standard errors — that measure how well a model fits,
never whether the form itself is right. Specification tests exist to catch wrong forms, and a growing literature
benchmarks them one gate at a time: which detector has the highest power, the best calibration, the cheapest reference
budget. This paper measures a different quantity, the one a deployed pipeline actually consumes: when the fitted
functional form is wrong, how often does every check stay quiet at once? We call this event joint silence — a fitted
model receives a certificate from the entire falsification panel: no specification gate rejects it, and no gate declines
evaluation. Across 171,000 simulated datasets built from physical laws — 19 controlled synthetic scenarios and 18
ground-truth equations from the SRSD-Feynman registry, at n  {50, 200, 1000} and relative noise from 0.5% to 5%,
evaluated by a four-gate panel (permutation curvature, RESET fitted-F, and two heteroskedasticity-robust RESET
variants on singular-value-safe paths) — the results separate sharply by provenance. On the synthetic gallery, where
scenarios were constructed around the gates' known sensitivity, joint silence on wrong forms is 0.2% at n = 50 and zero
at n ≥ 200: the panel is nearly closed by construction. On real physics equations, it is 37.8% at n = 50 and still 23.2% at
n = 1000 (noise-present strata). The baseline that makes these numbers interpretable has never been reported alongside
gate power tables: on true power laws the same panel stays jointly silent 87–88% of the time, so a silent certificate
carries a likelihood ratio of only 2.3–3.8, and P(form is wrong | all clear) remains above 0.20 at every sample size.
Silence concentrates exactly where single-gate maps predict: tasks whose misfit direction is near-orthogonal to every
tested column set reach 85% silence with no individual gate looking broken. And silence has a tail cost: 7.1% of silent
certificates at n = 50 carry far-field extrapolation error above 10%, a rate alarms would have flagged 7.6× more often.
Adding the strongest single-gate alternative from recent benchmarking — an AICc-adjudicated nested quadratic —
does not seal the leaks: on the physics bench the augmented panel still certifies 42% of wrong forms silent at n = 50.
The contribution is a measured semantics for falsification-side certification: passing the current toolbox is evidence of
consistency with the tested directions, not evidence of correctness, and panel-level coverage — not single-gate ranking
— is the quantity benchmark design should optimize.
1 Introduction
A regression model can be simultaneously excellent on every certification metric a discovery pipeline computes and
wrong in its functional form. A power law fitted to Prandtl–Karman friction data reaches R² = 0.99, passes k-fold
cross-validation, and reports small standard errors, while extrapolating with −20% error one decade outside the training
domain. The falsification-side response is well developed: Ramsey's RESET and its variants test whether powers of
fitted values or regressors carry residual signal; heteroskedasticity-robust covariance estimation hardens the inference;
and the workflow alternative — fit a nested rival family and let cross-validation or an information criterion arbitrate —
predates all of them. Recent work has benchmarked these gates seriously: matched decision bases, power compared at
calibrated false-positive rates, task pools drawn from real physical laws, failure registration. That literature is the
starting point of this paper, and its per-gate tables are what we re-read jointly.
That benchmarking tradition measures gates one at a time. Its output is a leaderboard: gate X beats gate Y by 8 points
of calibrated power on the physics bench; gate Y is the better default where a reference pool exists. The numbers are
real and the comparisons are fair. But they answer a question a pipeline does not face. A deployed discovery system
does not ship a model when its best gate objects; it ships a model when nothing objects. The quantity that governs
deployment is the joint event across the whole panel — and a joint event has no obligation to track the marginals. Four
gates each with 80% power can, if their failures correlate, leave half the wrong forms unobjected; the marginal tables
contain no row that reveals this.
This paper measures the joint event directly and gives it a name. Define joint silence on a dataset as: no specification
gate rejects at its threshold, and no gate declines evaluation — a registered refusal is an honest refusal, not a certificate,
and counting it as silence would flatter the panel. Joint silence on wrong-form data is the rate at which a falsification


===== PAGE 2 =====
panel certifies a wrong function as clean. It is, to our knowledge, unmeasured: every per-gate table in the benchmarking
literature answers "how often does this gate catch it," never "how often does anything catch it."
Two baselines give the quantity meaning, and both are absent from current practice. First, the wrong-form silence rate
itself, per bench. Second, the H0 silence rate — how often the same panel stays jointly quiet on true laws. The two
together convert silence into evidence: the ratio is a likelihood ratio, and its reciprocal structure gives P(form is wrong |
all clear) under a class mix. A panel that flags 30% of true laws but 25% of wrong ones certifies nothing; a panel quiet
on 88% of true laws and 38% of wrong ones certifies only weakly. Neither statement follows from any marginal power
table, however carefully calibrated.
We evaluate a four-gate panel — the permutation curvature gate (form_check), RESET fitted-F, and fitted/exog
HC3-Wald variants on singular value decomposition paths immune to the recently documented pinv(X′X) conditioning
hazard — on 19 synthetic scenarios and 18 SRSD-Feynman ground-truth equations, 171,000 wrong-form fits with
noise present in total, plus their 78,000+ true-law counterparts as the calibration baseline, with extrapolation ground
truth for every physics-bench fit. Three questions structure the paper:
 •  Q1. How often does the panel certify a wrong function, and does the
answer depend on where the wrong functions come from?
 •  Q2. What is a silent certificate worth — what likelihood ratio does
it carry, and how does that compare with what a reader of the single-gate literature would assume?
 •  Q3. Where silence concentrates, and what does it cost downstream?
The answers are uncomfortable and clean. The same panel, same thresholds, same noise: silence is a rounding error on
constructed scenarios (0.2% at n = 50, zero above) and better than one in three on real physics equations (37.8% at n =
50, 23.2% at n = 1000). Silent certificates carry likelihood ratios of 2.3–3.8 — weak evidence by the standards of
anything else we call a certificate. The silence sits exactly on the blind directions the single-gate literature has mapped:
odd-order curvature, cross-terms absent from every tested column set, tasks where all four marginal powers read
.04–.18 and nothing looks broken. And the tail is not safe: 7.1% of silent certificates at n = 50 hide far-field
extrapolation errors above 10%.
We also test the obvious repair — add the strongest single gates the recent benchmarking literature proposes (CV- and
AICc-adjudicated quadratic rivals, the documented winners among default detectors). They close the synthetic bench to
6% and leave the physics bench at 42% wrong-form silence in their own coordinates: the leaks live in directions no
tested column set spans, so panels do not become complete by becoming longer. What follows is not a ranking but a
semantics: a pass through the current falsification toolbox is evidence of consistency with the tested directions, nothing
stronger. Section 5 argues the consequence — that panel-level direction coverage, not single-gate superiority, is the
quantity benchmark design should optimize — and Section 6 states what we did not measure.
2 Related Work
Specification testing. RESET [1] augments a regression with powers of the fitted values and tests their joint
significance; built-regressor variants use powers of the regressors. Heteroskedasticity-consistent covariance estimation
[2] and its HC3 refinement [3, 4] are standard small-sample corrections, with a documented numerical hazard when the
HC3 sandwich is formed through pinv(X′X) on ill-conditioned designs — a failure of implementation, not of the
estimator. Our panel uses SVD-path HC3 throughout; the hazard is the reason, and the released paths register
conditioning per call.
The falsification-side benchmark. Symbolic regression benchmarks (SRBench [5], SRSD [6]) measure the discovery
side: whether a method finds the right expression. Neither measures whether an attached falsification layer would reject
a wrong form. The falsification-side benchmark literature supplies this: task pools from real physical laws, matched
decision bases, power at calibrated false-positive rates, failure registration, and — in its most recent revision — a
head-to-head against the nested-rival gates a reviewer would name, where an AICc-adjudicated quadratic expansion
won every cell as the default detector. That literature is this paper's direct predecessor, and its per-gate tables are what
this paper re-reads jointly. We inherit its protocol wholesale: one decision basis per comparison, H0-pool calibration,
preregistered labels, typed failure registration, seed rules that make any cell bit-reproducible.


===== PAGE 3 =====
Model selection as a gate. Comparing against a nested rival by AICc [12] or cross-validation [13] is the oldest
workflow-level specification check and the canonical objection to any new test. As a member of a panel it has different
geometry than as a soloist: the rival family defines directions the panel can see, and a wrong form orthogonal to those
directions is invisible to the member exactly as it is to the test statistics. Our augmented-panel experiment (§4.5)
measures this directly.
Resampling nulls. The permutation curvature gate's null is a residual permutation — an approximation under linear
models, empirically calibrated in log-log coordinates on every pool measured. We treat calibration as a measured
property, quote per-gate empirical sizes alongside every silence number, and claim no exactness.
What is missing. No prior falsification-side work reports a joint statistic: every published table is a per-gate marginal.
The omission is understandable — joint events need per-row agreement across gates on identical datasets, which
requires the seeded, per-cell release discipline only recently adopted in this area. It is also consequential: the joint
numbers reverse no marginal conclusion while adding the one quantity deployment actually consumes.
3 Design
3.1 The event, fixed before the run
The unit of analysis is a fitted dataset: one dataset, all four gates evaluated on it. The event of interest is fixed in the
analysis script before its first run:
> Joint silence = no gate rejects at its decision threshold AND no gate > declined evaluation. A declined evaluation —
degenerate residuals, rank > deficiency, insufficient degrees of freedom — is registered with a typed > reason by the
harness and counts as an honest refusal, not a certificate. > Counting refusals as silence would flatter the panel;
counting them as > detections would flatter it differently; we report refusal counts > separately and exclude them from
both numerator and denominator of the > silence rate. (In the runs below, refusals are rare: the four-gate > panels'
declined evaluations are 0 at all headline cells on both benches.)
Two threshold regimes are carried through every table: nominal (α = .05 for every gate, the deployed default) and
calibrated (each gate's threshold set to the 5th percentile of its H0 p-value distribution within the same bench and
sample-size stratum, giving each gate a 5% empirical false-positive rate). Levels differ by about one point everywhere;
conclusions are identical. The paper quotes nominal and flags the check.
3.2 Gates
The panel is the four-gate configuration current falsification-side practice deploys:
 •  A — permutation curvature. The form_check gate: residual-permutation
empirical p-value (B = 999) for curvature in log-log space beyond a linear power law. Self-calibrating in the sense that
statistic and null share one fit.
 •  B — RESET fitted-F. Ramsey's test with powers of fitted values,
classical F reference.
 •  C — RESET fitted-HC3 (SVD path). The same augmentation with
heteroskedasticity-robust Wald inference, covariance formed through the singular value decomposition of the design
— not pinv(X′X), whose squared condition number silently destroys the statistic on few-decade physical designs.
 •  D — RESET exog-HC3 (SVD path). The built-regressor augmentation
(powers of each regressor), same robust SVD inference.
The A/B paths are unchanged from the audited archive (A's permutation is row-self-consistent; B uses RSS and never
touched the broken path); C/D use the corrected paths throughout. Per-gate empirical sizes on the H0 pools are quoted
in §7 and run .049–.060 on the physics bench and .046–.068 on the synthetic bench across strata.
3.3 Suites
S1 — synthetic gallery. Ground-truth mean functions are prespecified: three strict power laws (H0) and eleven
structural alternatives entering the wrong-form pool — quadratic, cubic, interaction, omitted correlated variable,
dilution, opposing curvature, rational saturation, exponential, logarithmic, threshold, and a collinear-quadratic stress


===== PAGE 4 =====
case — plus four noise-model variants carried for semantics but excluded here (they are wrong-error-model, not
wrong-form). Designs span log-uniform domains of ~4.6 decades; noise is multiplicative Gaussian at relative scale rel
 {0.5%, 1%, 3%, 5%} (plus a zero-noise stratum that exercises failure registration: exact fits emit no certificates, and
zero-noise cells are excluded from silence rates by construction). n  {50, 200, 1000}; 500 seeds per cell; 66,000
wrong-form datasets with noise present.
S2 — SRSD-Feynman equations (18 tasks). From the 120-equation registry, tasks passing the positivity screen under
official sampling configurations: 13 strict-monomial H0 and 19 non-monomial H1 candidates, one of which is
excluded here (a reproduction sentinel flagged its archived p-values as non-reproducible under regeneration, leaving 18
wrong-form equations). Each fit (n  {50, 200, 1000} × rel grid × 500 seeds) carries a 2,000-sample same-distribution
test set and a far-field probe: the first regressor extrapolated ×3 beyond both training domain edges, scored against the
closed-form truth in a numerically stable log-difference form. This gives every wrong-form fit an extrapolation ground
truth — the ingredient that makes silence's downstream cost measurable rather than rhetorical.
S5 — the nested-rival augmentation. To test the obvious repair we reuse the gate-comparison suite: the same seeds
evaluated additionally by CV-quad (5-fold, calibrated threshold δ) and AICc-quad (calibrated m), the documented
strongest default detectors, with AICc-quad as the representative winner. Its grid is rel = 3%, n  {50, 200}, 200 seeds;
silence levels on this grid are internally comparable but not cross-comparable to the S2 pool levels at other noise scales,
and we flag every such comparison.
3.4 Why these two suites are the right pair
The synthetic gallery was constructed around known gate sensitivities — its alternatives are curves, interactions, and
thresholds chosen because panels were expected to see them. The Feynman equations were constructed by physicists,
for physics, with no gate in mind. Any gap between the two benches' joint-silence rates is therefore itself a finding: it
measures how much of a panel's apparent competence is an artifact of who chose the alternatives. This is the same logic
that separates a validation set from a test set, applied to falsification-side benchmarking.
3.5 What we did not measure
Observational data (macroeconomic relations, engineering bench cells) is excluded: joint silence is defined against
ground truth, and observational cells have none — their rejection counts are compatibility statistics, not rates. The
battery and family-routing machinery is excluded: it answers a different question (what to escalate to), and its null is
rebuilt per coordinate family. Other panel configurations (wild-bootstrap nulls, leverage-adjusted variants) are future
work; the released harness is gate-agnostic and the joint statistic is one reduction over per-cell rows.

the union of direction sets, and the gates available off-the-shelf — RESET variants, robust Wald, quadratic rivals —
draw their columns from the same second-order vocabulary. What a pass certifies is consistency with that vocabulary;
§4.2's likelihood ratios are the honest exchange rate.
5 Analysis
Q1. Does the panel certify wrong functions? Yes — at a rate that depends on who chose the wrong functions.
Constructed alternatives: 0.2% and below. Physicists' alternatives: 38% at n = 50, 23% at n = 1000. The distribution is
not a tail phenomenon; it is the bulk of the physics-bench mass, concentrated in six of eighteen tasks. Any deployment
that reads "no gate objected" as evidence of correctness is importing an assumption the panel's own numbers do not
support.
Q2. Is a silent certificate worth anything? Yes, and quantifying the "yes" is the point. LR 2.3–3.8 is real evidence —
better than nothing, monotone in n, stable under calibrated thresholds. But it is three-fold odds on a decision (deploy or
not) where the losses are asymmetric and the alternatives include collecting more data. The certificate's information
content is better understood as a floor on uncertainty than a license: P(wrong | clear) ≥ .21 at every n we measured,
under the friendliest class mix. Nothing in the marginal tables would have led a reader to expect weakness of this
magnitude — the best marginal power (.48 for gate D at n = 50, .72 at n = 1000) is respectable, and marginal power is
what the literature reports.
Q3. Where should silence be expected? Where the misfit direction is orthogonal to the tested column vocabulary:
oscillating curvature (trigonometric laws), interaction structures absent from every augmentation, near-linear-in-log
regimes whose curvature is second-order in-domain but first-order far-field. The per-gate literature mapped these as its
blind spots; the joint analysis shows they compound — same tasks, same datasets, all gates together. A practitioner can


===== PAGE 8 =====
therefore predict silence from structure before running anything: if the residual story of a candidate form is "curvature
that oscillates" or "a cross-term," a four- or five-gate panel will not save it.
Q4. What follows for benchmark design? Single-gate leaderboards optimize a quantity no pipeline consumes. The
design target is panel-level: maximize the likelihood ratio of silence (drive wrong-form silence down against the H0
silence budget), which means adding direction sets, not gates — the augmented-panel experiment shows
same-vocabulary additions spend false-positive budget far faster than they add coverage. Concretely: a benchmark's
next axis is not "which test wins" but "what direction vocabulary closes the physics-bench leaks" — candidates include
the smooth-test and conditional-moment literature (Neyman-style data-driven directions [21, 22], integrated
conditional moments [23]), oscillation-sensitive bases for trigonometric laws, and interaction-aware rival families.
Each is one statistic-plus-decision function over the released per-cell rows; the harness is gate-agnostic and the joint
statistic is one reduction.
6 Limitations
Provenance of the event definition. The joint event was fixed in the analysis script before its first run, but the decision
to compute it followed the per-gate analyses; it is a post hoc reduction over released per-cell rows, not a prespecified
analysis of a prespecified panel. We regard the event definition as forced (any definition counting refusals as silence, or
flagging any single gate as the certificate, answers a different question), but the reporting-only degree of freedom is
real and deserves the same scrutiny rigorous benchmarking elsewhere applies. The calibrated-threshold variant is
released alongside every nominal number as the check.
Panel and threshold dependence. Results are for one four-gate panel at one significance level (with one calibrated
variant); other panels, other α, and per-gate operating points tuned for recall will shift the rates. The likelihood-ratio
reading is internal to the bench's class mix; we quote LR as the mix-free quantity and P(wrong | clear) only under the
stated 50/50 mix.
Positive dependence is the mechanism, not a confound. The gates share training data and overlapping null
constructions; their errors are positively dependent, which is exactly why H0 silence (87–88%) exceeds what
independent 5% budgets would give (~81% under independence) and why adding same-vocabulary gates fails. We do
not "correct" for the dependence; it is the finding.
Extrapolation metric. The far-field probe is ×3 beyond the training log-range (half a decade) along the first regressor,
scored log-difference-stable. It is one metric of downstream wrongness; design- and control-relevant harms will track
different tails. The silent-fit tail we measure (7.1% above 10% at n = 50) is a floor on the deployment cost of silence,
not a ceiling.
Withdrawn task. One SRSD equation is excluded under a reproduction-sentinel protocol (its archived p-values do not
regenerate under bit-identical conditions; the other 31 tasks reproduce to ≥ 6 digits). Its exclusion changes pool
membership, not method; the released per-task table allows any reader to recompute silence on any task subset.
Post hoc pool. S5's grid (rel = 3%, n  {50, 200}, 200 seeds) was designed for single-gate comparison; we reuse it for
the augmented panel rather than re-running a dedicated grid. Levels on that grid are not comparable to the S2 pool
levels at other noise scales, and every such comparison is flagged in place.
What is not claimed. No claim that the four gates are the right panel — only that it is the panel current practice
deploys. No claim that silence causes extrapolation harm — the conditional table is a measured association on
simulated ground truth, in the direction the mechanism predicts. No transfer claim to observational data.
7 Reproducibility
Every number in this paper reduces over released per-cell rows under fixed seed rules: dataset generation is seeded
from a documented rule embedding the grid position, gate evaluation is deterministic given the dataset (A's
permutation seed is the dataset seed), and the joint statistic is a pure function of the released p-value columns. The
analysis script fixes the event definition (§3.1), both threshold regimes, the task pool (18 equations after the
documented withdrawal), and the refusal-exclusion rule, before any reduction; it writes per-task and per-scenario
silence tables, the H0 baselines, the conditional-extrapolation tables, and the augmented-panel tables, with both
threshold variants side by side. Wilson intervals for headline silence rates are ±.004 or smaller; the honest uncertainty is
